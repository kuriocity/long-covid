## Minoxidil — the only proven regrowth drug

### Mechanism

Minoxidil is a **prodrug**. It must be sulfated to **minoxidil sulfate** by **sulfotransferase (SULT1A1)** in the hair follicle's outer root sheath to do anything. Minoxidil sulfate opens ATP-sensitive potassium channels → vasodilation, prolonged anagen, larger dermal papilla.

**This is why ~30–50% of people are minoxidil "non-responders": they have low follicular SULT1A1 activity.** It's an enzyme problem, not a compliance problem, and it's addressable — see tretinoin below.

### Topical vs oral

| | **Topical 5%** | **Low-dose oral (LDOM)** |
|---|---|---|
| Dosing | Twice daily, 1 mL | **Once daily, one tablet** |
| Approval | FDA-approved OTC | Off-label (approved as an antihypertensive at much higher doses) |
| Evidence | Multiple RCTs, decades | Cohorts + single-arm meta-analysis + 2024/2025 expert consensus. Large RCTs pending. |
| Bypasses SULT1A1 problem? | No — depends on follicular sulfotransferase | Partially — hepatic sulfation contributes, so some topical non-responders respond to oral |
| Scalp irritation / flaking | Common (propylene glycol) | None |
| Hair texture / greasiness | The main reason people quit | None |
| Systemic AEs | Minimal | Hypertrichosis ~24% (face, body — dose-dependent), transient shedding 16–22%, mild peripheral edema ~2%, occasional tachycardia/lightheadedness |
| Contraindications | Few | Pericardial disease, uncontrolled hypertension, pregnancy |

### LDOM dosing (per 2024–2025 expert consensus)

- Consensus range for off-label alopecia use: **0.625–5 mg/day**
- Typical male AGA start: **1.25 mg/day**, or 2.5 mg/day for adults 18+
- Escalate to **2.5 mg/day** after 2–3 months if tolerated
- Optional further escalation to **5 mg/day** (the stated maximum) after another 2–3 months
- Requires a prescription and a physician who will monitor BP/HR and edema

### The adherence argument for going oral

Twice-daily topical minoxidil has among the worst real-world adherence of any dermatological therapy: it's a 2×/day, wet, greasy, texture-ruining, indefinitely-repeated task with no immediate reward and a visible penalty for stopping only 4 months later. Discontinuation and long gaps are the norm, not the exception.

**If your actual failure mode has been inconsistency rather than non-response, switching to once-daily oral minoxidil is likely a bigger real-world improvement than any drug-potency upgrade.** A 1.25 mg tablet taken every morning with existing medication beats a 5% solution applied when you remember. This is the single highest-leverage change available to an inconsistent topical user.

### Shedding on start ("dread shed")

Both minoxidil and 5-ARIs can trigger a synchronised telogen release in weeks 2–8. Reported in 16–22% of LDOM users. It means the drug is acting on the cycle. **Do not stop.** It resolves by ~month 3.

## Tretinoin — the minoxidil enabler (the "magic" claim, assessed)

Tretinoin comes up constantly in hair-loss communities. Here's what the evidence actually supports.

### What's real

- **Tretinoin increases follicular SULT1A1 activity ~1.8× within 5 days** (Sharma et al., Dermatologic Therapy 2019)
- In that study, **43% of patients predicted to be minoxidil non-responders were converted into responders** by adding tretinoin
- Tretinoin also increases minoxidil **penetration** — pharmacokinetic data show a **nearly 3-fold increase in systemic minoxidil absorption** when tretinoin cream is applied before minoxidil

### What's not established

- A 2025/2026 Karger review of tretinoin + minoxidil cotherapy concluded that **clinical combination data are minimal** and current evidence **does not support first-line dual therapy**
- The conclusion it does support: **tretinoin is a reasonable adjunct, especially in patients not responding to topical minoxidil**

### How to use it, if you use it

- **Tretinoin 0.025% (start low)**, applied to the scalp at night, **3× per week** initially, then increase frequency only if no irritation
- Apply tretinoin first, let it absorb, then minoxidil — or better, alternate: tretinoin at night, minoxidil in the morning, to reduce irritation and reduce the systemic-absorption spike
- Expect irritation, flaking, and photosensitivity. Titrate slowly. Combining tretinoin with an already-irritating propylene-glycol minoxidil solution is how people end up with a dermatitis and quit both
- **Do not add tretinoin if you're on oral minoxidil.** The mechanism is follicular sulfotransferase and penetration of a *topical*. There's no rationale, and no data, for pairing it with LDOM.

**Verdict: not magic. A legitimate rescue for topical minoxidil non-response, with a real mechanism and one supportive trial. Correct position in the sequence is after a fair trial of minoxidil has underdelivered — not at the start.**

## Microneedling — the best cost-to-benefit item on the list

### Evidence

2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (Archives of Dermatological Research), 12 RCTs, 631 AGA patients:

- Combined microneedling + minoxidil vs minoxidil alone: **SMD 1.32 for hair count (95% CI 0.73–1.92, p < 0.01)**
- **Heterogeneity I² = 88%** — the direction of effect is robust, the magnitude is genuinely uncertain
- Benefit held **across all tested needle depths** and both short and long treatment durations
- Depth, duration and technique variation did **not** significantly change the outcome

That last point is practically useful: you don't need to optimise the protocol. You need to do it.

### Protocol

- **Depth 1.0–1.5 mm** (deeper is not better and increases risk)
- **Once weekly to once fortnightly** — allow full healing between sessions
- Clean scalp, disinfected roller/pen, replace cartridges/rollers regularly
- **Do not apply minoxidil or tretinoin immediately after** — wait ~24 hours. Applying an irritant into fresh channels causes contact dermatitis and, with minoxidil, a large absorption spike
- Cost: a derma pen is a one-time low-cost purchase. This is the cheapest evidence-supported intervention available

## Ketoconazole 2% shampoo — free upside

- Systematic review (2020) and a 2025 review: increased **hair shaft diameter** and **pilary index** (% anagen × diameter); adjunct benefit alongside finasteride
- Mechanism: reduces perifollicular inflammation and *Malassezia* load; weak local androgen-receptor antagonism
- **Protocol:** 2–3× per week, lather, **leave on 3–5 minutes**, rinse. Contact time is the whole point — a 10-second wash does nothing. Use a normal conditioner after (it's drying)
- Cost: trivial. Risk: trivial. The only reason not to do it is that the effect is small.

## Things that are marketed as adjuncts and mostly aren't

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| **Topical melatonin** | Significantly inferior to minoxidil 5% on 24-week hair count. Only sensible for minoxidil-intolerant patients. |
| **Caffeine shampoos/serums** | Mechanistically plausible, clinically limited and mixed. Not a treatment. |
| **Rosemary oil** | Network meta-analyses put it below conventional agents. Harmless, not a substitute. |
| **Saw palmetto** | Weak 5-AR inhibition on low-quality data — **and it shares finasteride's neurosteroid mechanism.** Not a "safe natural alternative." |
| **Biotin** | Only helps if deficient. Also **skews thyroid and troponin assays** — stop 3 days before any bloodwork. |
| **Peptide/growth-factor serums, exosome topicals** | No adequate RCTs. Priced as if there were. |
| **Dermarolling alone** | The evidence is for microneedling *with minoxidil*. Alone, it's not a treatment. |

## The stack, assembled

A complete, evidence-supported, self-administered regimen:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Every morning | 5-ARI (oral, with food, same time daily) + LDOM if using oral minoxidil |
| Every morning + night | Minoxidil 5% topical, 1 mL — **if** using topical instead of oral |
| 2–3× per week | Ketoconazole 2% shampoo, 3–5 min contact |
| Weekly or fortnightly | Microneedling 1.0–1.5 mm (no topicals for 24 h after) |
| 3× per week at night, *only if topical minoxidil is underperforming* | Tretinoin 0.025% |
| Every 3 months | Standardised photo set |
| Weekly | Side-effect + mood/anhedonia self-rating |

Total cost of that entire stack, generics, India market: roughly **₹500–1,500/month**. The expensive items (PRP, transplant) are in [05-procedures.md](05-procedures.md) and are optional.

# 03 — The 5-ARI Guide: Finasteride vs Dutasteride

This is the decision that determines the outcome. Everything else is a modifier.

---

## Head-to-head

| | **Finasteride** | **Dutasteride** |
|---|---|---|
| Isoenzymes inhibited | 5-AR **type 2** only | 5-AR **type 1 + type 2** |
| Serum DHT suppression | ~70–71% | ~90–98% |
| Scalp DHT suppression | ~40–50% | ~50–55% |
| Standard AGA dose | 1 mg/day | 0.5 mg/day |
| Plasma half-life | ~6–8 hours | **~5 weeks** (terminal, at steady state) |
| Regulatory status for AGA | FDA-approved | Approved in South Korea & Japan; **off-label** elsewhere |
| Efficacy on hair count | Reference | **Superior** (2019 SR/MA; ranked #1 monotherapy in 2025 network MA) |
| Adverse event rates in trials | Sexual AEs ~2–4% vs ~2% placebo | **Similar rates**, including sexual dysfunction (per 2019 SR/MA) |
| Washout after stopping | Days | **Months** |

### The honest summary

Dutasteride is **more effective**. The 2019 systematic review/meta-analysis and the 2025 network meta-analysis both support this, and dutasteride 0.5 mg/day ranks as the single most effective monotherapy available for male AGA. Trial-level adverse event rates — including sexual dysfunction — are **similar** between the two drugs.

But two asymmetries make dutasteride the higher-variance choice:

1. **Type 1 inhibition includes the brain.** 5-AR type 1 is the isoenzyme expressed in the CNS and responsible for converting progesterone to neuroactive steroids. Dutasteride suppresses it; finasteride largely does not. If someone experiences neuropsychiatric effects, dutasteride's mechanism to do so is broader. See [07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md](07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md).
2. **The 5-week half-life cuts both ways.** It forgives missed doses — but if you develop a side effect, you cannot get out quickly. Finasteride clears in days; dutasteride takes months.

**Choose dutasteride if** maximum efficacy is the priority, you tolerate it, and you're not actively investigating a mood/anhedonia signal.
**Choose finasteride if** you want the better-characterised safety profile, a fast exit if needed, or you're trying to attribute a neuropsychiatric symptom to something.

> ### ⚠️ Correction (2026-08-24) — the comparative epidemiology does not support "dutasteride is worse for mood"
>
> Earlier versions of this document (and [07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md](07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md)) argued from mechanism that dutasteride should carry more neuropsychiatric liability than finasteride, because it inhibits **type 1** — the CNS isoenzyme — while finasteride largely does not. That reasoning is sound in principle. **The comparative human data does not bear it out, and may point the other way.**
>
> - A **French nationwide cohort study** (men ≥50 initiating finasteride 5 mg or dutasteride 0.5 mg, 2012–2016; outcome = suicide death or hospitalisation for self-harm) found **increased risk with finasteride relative to dutasteride** among men with prior mood disorders. The authors note this is equally consistent with either increased risk from finasteride or a protective effect of dutasteride
> - **Pharmacovigilance analysis** reports psychopathological issues **more frequently for finasteride than dutasteride**
> - A **South Korean cohort** found no difference in depression risk between finasteride and alpha-blocker users after propensity-score matching
>
> **Caveats that matter:** these are BPH populations aged 50+, using **finasteride 5 mg — five times the AGA dose** — and they are observational, with confounding by indication. None of this is AGA-population data at 1 mg. So it does not prove finasteride is worse either.
>
> **What it does establish: a lateral switch from oral dutasteride to oral finasteride is not a reliable way to reduce mood risk.** Mechanistic plausibility was the entire basis for that recommendation, and the epidemiology does not support it.
>
> **Revised position: if the concern is a 5-ARI mood effect, the step that actually reduces exposure is a change of route or dose — topical finasteride 0.25%, or a structured holiday — not swapping one oral 5-ARI for another.**

## Dosing, and the pharmacokinetics that everyone gets wrong

### Dutasteride's half-life makes daily dosing unnecessary

- Single 0.5–5 mg doses clear with a 3–4 day half-life
- Repeated dosing saturates the rapid elimination pathway → terminal half-life stretches to **~5 weeks** at steady state
- Consequence: **serum DHT stays suppressed between doses.** A weekly 3.5 mg dose produces a serum DHT of roughly 20 ng/mL — close to what daily 0.5 mg produces
- Missed doses cause **minimal DHT rebound**
- Many hair-restoration physicians therefore dose it **twice weekly** off-label

### The critical corollary — read this if you are "reducing" dutasteride

**Alternate-day dutasteride is not a meaningful dose reduction.** With a 5-week terminal half-life, alternate-day dosing (≈1.75 mg/week) sits at or above the exposure that produces near-maximal DHT suppression. In DHT terms you are, for practical purposes, still on full dutasteride.

So if the goal of reducing frequency was to reduce a **DHT-mediated or neurosteroid-mediated side effect**, alternate-day dosing does not achieve it. It reduces pill count, not drug effect. The genuine de-escalation ladder is:

```
Dutasteride 0.5 mg daily            ~90-98% serum DHT suppression
Dutasteride 0.5 mg alt-day/3x wk    ~ still near-maximal (NOT a real reduction)
        ↓ real step down
Finasteride 1 mg daily              ~71% serum DHT suppression, type 2 only, days to washout
        ↓ real step down
Finasteride 1 mg every other day    ~ still substantial suppression (t½ short but effect outlasts it)
        ↓ real step down
Topical finasteride 0.25%           ~34.5% serum DHT reduction, comparable local efficacy
        ↓ real step down
No 5-ARI                            progression resumes; gains lost over 6-12 months
```

### 2025 intermittent-dosing RCT

A randomized, investigator-blinded pilot (JAAD International, 2025) compared **twice-weekly** and **thrice-weekly dutasteride** against **daily finasteride 1 mg**:

- Hair density and diameter increased significantly in **all** groups
- Dose-dependent effect across the dutasteride arms
- **Thrice-weekly dutasteride did not significantly beat daily finasteride 1 mg** on hair count
- Thrice-weekly *was* superior to twice-weekly

Read: intermittent dutasteride is legitimate and works. It is not automatically better than daily finasteride at reduced frequency — so if you're dosing dutasteride intermittently, you're roughly in finasteride's efficacy territory while retaining dutasteride's type-1 inhibition and long washout. **If you're going to dose intermittently anyway, daily finasteride is a defensible swap with a cleaner side-effect and exit profile.**

## Adverse effects — the real numbers

### Sexual

- Trial rates: ~2–4% for finasteride vs ~2% placebo; similar for dutasteride
- Most cases resolve on discontinuation, and a substantial fraction resolve on continuation
- Real-world reporting rates are higher than trial rates. Nocebo is demonstrably a large component (studies where patients are not told about sexual side effects report far fewer), **but that does not mean every case is nocebo**

### Neuropsychiatric — the 2025 regulatory shift

This changed recently and matters:

- **2025: The European Medicines Agency confirmed finasteride is associated with suicidal ideation.** The FDA and MHRA both moved to strengthen label warnings.
- Pharmacovigilance analyses have detected depression and suicidality signals for **both** finasteride and dutasteride, with recommendations for psychiatric assessment before and during treatment
- A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis examined depression risk with attention to comparator-group choice — a real methodological problem in this literature, since BPH and AGA control groups differ substantially
- Proposed mechanism: 5-AR inhibition blocks conversion of progesterone → 5α-dihydroprogesterone → **allopregnanolone**, a potent positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A. Lower allopregnanolone is independently associated with depressed mood, anxiety and anhedonia.

**Post-finasteride syndrome (PFS)** describes persistent symptoms after discontinuation: decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, **sexual anhedonia**, cognitive impairment, **fatigue**, anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation. Its existence as a distinct entity is contested; its symptom reports are not fabricated. Incidence is unknown and probably low, but it is the reason to take an emerging mood symptom on a 5-ARI seriously rather than waiting it out.

### Other

- Gynecomastia / breast tenderness: uncommon, reversible, report it
- Reduced ejaculate volume: common, benign
- **PSA suppression:** 5-ARIs roughly halve PSA. Any future PSA result must be **doubled** for interpretation. Tell any doctor ordering a PSA that you're on a 5-ARI — this has caused missed prostate cancer diagnoses.
- Fertility: reduced sperm parameters in a minority, generally reversible. If actively trying to conceive, discuss a washout — remembering dutasteride's is months, not days.

## Monitoring protocol

| What | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Standardised photos | Baseline, then every 3 months | The only honest efficacy measure |
| Libido / erectile function / mood self-rating (1–10) | Weekly, same day of week | Catches a trend before it becomes a crisis. Retrospective recall is unreliable and biased by expectation. |
| Anhedonia rating (1–10) | Weekly | Specifically relevant given the LC overlap |
| Total + free testosterone, LH | Baseline, ~6 months | Baseline is essential — without it, any later value is uninterpretable |
| PSA | Baseline if 40+, then per age guidelines | Establishes the pre-suppression reference |
| LFTs | Baseline, once at 6–12 months | Both drugs are hepatically metabolised; routine monitoring isn't mandated but baseline is cheap |

**Rule: change one variable at a time, and leave 8+ weeks between changes.** With dutasteride's 5-week half-life, changes made closer together than that are uninterpretable — you will not know what caused what.

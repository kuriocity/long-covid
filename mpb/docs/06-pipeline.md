# 06 — The 2026 Pipeline: What's Coming and When

Everything here is **not available to you now**. The point of this document is calibration: to make it obvious that waiting for the pipeline is not a strategy, and to know which one item is worth actually tracking.

**The structural fact:** AGA treatment in 2026 has exactly two tiers — what's available now (minoxidil, finasteride/dutasteride, LLLT, PRP, surgery) and what's coming. Nothing new has become available in over two decades. Minoxidil was approved in 1988, finasteride in 1997.

---

## Near-term: likely available 2027–2028

### Clascoterone 5% topical solution — the one to watch

- **Mechanism:** topical **androgen receptor antagonist**. Blocks the receptor locally rather than suppressing DHT systemically. Rapidly metabolised to an inactive metabolite in circulation → minimal systemic androgen effect
- **Status:** among the most advanced AGA programs; **FDA submissions targeted in 2026**, potential approval as early as **2027**
- **Why it matters here specifically:** it attacks the same pathway as a 5-ARI at the follicle, without systemic DHT suppression and therefore **without the neurosteroid/allopregnanolone mechanism** that drives the 5-ARI mood question. For anyone who has had to trade hair against mood on a 5-ARI, this is the single most relevant molecule in development
- The topical formulation (Breezula) is the AGA program; clascoterone is already approved as a topical for acne (Winlevi), which de-risks the safety profile considerably

### Veradermics oral minoxidil (extended-release)

- Purpose-built low-dose oral minoxidil product for AGA rather than off-label use of an antihypertensive
- Estimated **2027–2028**
- Practical impact: legitimises and standardises what's already being done off-label. Not a new mechanism

## Mid-term: 2029–2031

### GT20029 — topical PROTAC androgen receptor degrader (Kintor)

- **Mechanism:** first-in-class topical **PROTAC** (proteolysis-targeting chimera). Doesn't block the androgen receptor — **tags it for destruction** by the cell's own proteasome. Degradation rather than antagonism means the effect outlasts the drug's presence, enabling infrequent dosing
- **Phase 2 results published December 2025** (*Journal of Dermatological Treatment*): 0.5% once-daily and **1.0% twice-weekly** both significantly improved hair density versus placebo; hair width also improved significantly in the 1.0% twice-weekly arm. Well tolerated
- **Phase 3 not yet initiated.** Earliest realistic approval **~2029–2031**
- The **once- or twice-weekly topical dosing** is the standout feature — it directly attacks the adherence problem that limits topical therapy

### Pyrilutamide (KX-826) — topical AR antagonist (Kintor)

- **Met its Phase 3 primary endpoint** in a **666-patient trial in China**
- Potential **China commercialisation ~2027**; **US availability no earlier than ~2030**, since separate FDA trials would be required
- Earlier US Phase 2 results were mixed, which is why the China-first path
- Watch for grey-market availability once it launches in China — and note that buying it that way means no quality assurance

### PP405 — follicle activator (Pelage Pharmaceuticals)

- **Mechanism:** targets **mitochondrial pyruvate metabolism** in dormant hair-follicle stem cells to reactivate them. This is a genuinely different category — **not an anti-androgen at all**. It aims to *restart* follicles rather than protect them
- Early clinical results generated real enthusiasm; larger trials ongoing
- Estimated **2028–2031**
- **This is the closest thing to a "cure" candidate**, because it's the only mechanism aimed at the dormant-stem-cell problem rather than the androgen signal. It's also therefore the highest-risk: reactivating follicles that remain androgen-sensitive may require concurrent AR blockade to hold the gain

### Others in earlier development

- **HMI-115** (prolactin receptor antibody) — non-androgen mechanism, injectable, early phase
- **Verteporfin** — repurposed photosensitiser investigated for scarless wound healing / follicle neogenesis. Mechanistically interesting, **no AGA trial results identified as of 2026** — treat all claims about it as preclinical/theoretical
- Various **follicle-neogenesis** and cell-therapy programs — all early, all repeatedly over-promised for two decades

## The conclusion this document exists to deliver

**Do not wait.** The nearest genuinely new option is roughly **12–18 months away at best** (clascoterone), and the transformative ones are **3–5+ years out** — with the usual attrition rate, meaning some won't arrive at all.

Meanwhile, follicles are crossing the miniaturised → fibrosed line every month, and **that transition is not reversible by any of these drugs either.** Clascoterone, GT20029 and pyrilutamide are all androgen-pathway blockers: they will protect what you still have in 2027, and do nothing for what you lost in 2026. Even PP405 works on *dormant* follicles, not absent ones.

**The pipeline is a reason to preserve follicles now, not a reason to wait.** Every follicle you keep alive with today's imperfect drugs is a follicle that's still there to benefit when the better drug lands.

**One thing worth actively tracking:** clascoterone. Set a reminder for **2027-01** to check its FDA status. If it's approved and it works, it is a plausible route to the anti-androgen benefit without the systemic 5-ARI trade-off — see [07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md](07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md).

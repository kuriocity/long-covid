# 01 — Mechanism: Why the Hair Goes

## The one-sentence version

Genetically susceptible follicles express androgen receptors that, when bound by locally produced DHT, shorten the growth phase with every cycle — so each hair grows back thinner and shorter than the last, until it's invisible, and eventually until the follicle scars over.

---

## The four components

### 1. Genetic susceptibility (the part you inherited)

AGA is **polygenic** with a dominant contribution from the *AR/EDA2R* locus on the **X chromosome** — which is why maternal-line inheritance is classically emphasized, but ~250+ additional autosomal loci mean **paternal-line inheritance is equally real**. A father and grandfather with the same pattern is a strong predictor of both:

- **Earlier onset** than population average
- **Faster progression** if untreated
- **Similar final pattern** — the family Norwood endpoint is a reasonable prediction of your own untreated endpoint

What genetics does *not* determine: how much you keep. That's determined by how early and how consistently the androgen signal is suppressed. Genetics sets the slope; treatment sets the intercept and can flatten the slope.

### 2. Local androgen conversion

- Testosterone → **DHT** via **5-alpha-reductase (5-AR)**
- Two relevant isoenzymes: **type 1** (skin, sebaceous glands, liver, brain) and **type 2** (dermal papilla of the hair follicle, prostate, genital skin)
- DHT binds the androgen receptor with roughly **5× the affinity** of testosterone
- Key point: **this happens inside the scalp**, not in the bloodstream. Men with AGA typically have *normal* serum testosterone. This is a local sensitivity disease, not a hormone-excess disease.

Therapeutic implication: finasteride inhibits **type 2 only** (~71% serum DHT suppression); dutasteride inhibits **type 1 and 2** (~90–98%). That difference is the entire efficacy gap between them — and also the entire side-effect-profile question, because **type 1 is the isoenzyme active in the brain**.

### 3. Miniaturization — the actual mechanism of loss

The hair cycle: **anagen** (growth, normally 2–7 years) → **catagen** (regression, ~2 weeks) → **telogen** (rest, ~3 months) → shed → new anagen.

In an androgen-sensitized follicle, DHT–AR signaling:

- **Shortens anagen** — less time to grow, so shorter maximum length
- **Lengthens telogen** — more scalp visible at any moment
- **Shrinks the dermal papilla** — smaller papilla → smaller hair shaft diameter
- **Reduces pigmentation** — terminal → intermediate → vellus

Each cycle ratchets one step down. This is **why treatment takes 6–12 months to show**: you're not growing hair, you're waiting for the *next cycle* to be a better cycle than the last one.

```
Terminal hair (60-100 µm)
      ↓  cycle
Intermediate (40-60 µm)      ← still fully recoverable
      ↓  cycle
Vellus (<30 µm, unpigmented) ← recoverable, harder
      ↓  cycle
Follicle fibrosis / dropout  ← NOT recoverable by any drug
```

**The single most important clinical fact in this whole document:** drugs recover miniaturized follicles. They do not recover fibrosed ones. Every month untreated moves some follicles across a line that can't be crossed back. This is why "start now, imperfectly" beats "start later, optimally."

### 4. Secondary inflammation and fibrosis

Perifollicular **micro-inflammation** and lymphocytic infiltrate is present in a substantial fraction of AGA scalp biopsies, followed by perifollicular fibrosis. It's likely an accelerant rather than the primary driver, but it's a legitimate secondary target — which is the mechanistic rationale for:

- **Ketoconazole 2%** (anti-inflammatory + weak local AR antagonism)
- **Microneedling** (controlled wound healing → Wnt/β-catenin activation, growth factor release)
- **PRP** (concentrated platelet growth factors → dermal papilla stimulation)

### 5. The Wnt/β-catenin axis (why the pipeline looks the way it does)

Follicle regeneration is governed by **Wnt/β-catenin** signaling in the dermal papilla and bulge stem cells. AGA follicles retain their stem cells but lose the signal to activate them — the follicle is dormant, not absent, for a long window. This is the target of the genuinely novel pipeline agents (PP405 and follicle-neogenesis approaches), and it's why they represent a *different category* of drug than anything currently on the market: they aim to restart follicles rather than protect them.

---

## Why the crown/vertex specifically

Androgen receptor and 5-AR type 2 density is highest in the **frontal, temporal and vertex** scalp and lowest in the **occipital and lower parietal** scalp. That gradient is:

- Why the pattern is a pattern (the horseshoe is the androgen-insensitive zone)
- Why hair transplants work at all — donor follicles from the occipital "safe zone" **retain their androgen insensitivity after transplantation** (donor dominance)
- Why the vertex responds *better* to finasteride in trials than the frontal hairline does — most 5-ARI RCTs used vertex hair counts as the primary endpoint, and frontal response is consistently more modest

---

## What the mechanism implies for treatment design

| Mechanistic step | Drug/intervention that targets it | Category |
|---|---|---|
| Genetic susceptibility | Nothing available | — |
| T → DHT conversion | Finasteride (type 2), dutasteride (type 1+2) | **Halt** |
| DHT binding to AR | Clascoterone, pyrilutamide, GT20029 (all pipeline) | **Halt** (future) |
| Shortened anagen | Minoxidil (prolongs anagen, vasodilation, K+ channel opening) | **Regrow** |
| Minoxidil activation | Tretinoin (↑ SULT1A1) | **Enabler** |
| Poor drug penetration | Microneedling | **Enabler** |
| Perifollicular inflammation | Ketoconazole 2%, microneedling | **Adjunct** |
| Growth factor deficit | PRP, LLLT | **Adjunct** |
| Dormant stem cells | PP405, follicle neogenesis (pipeline) | **Cure candidate** |
| Fibrosed follicles gone | Hair transplant | **Restore** |

**The design rule that falls out of this:** you need at minimum one *Halt* agent and one *Regrow* agent running simultaneously. A Halt agent alone preserves; a Regrow agent alone loses ground to ongoing miniaturization. Adjuncts and enablers modify the size of the effect but never substitute for the two core legs.

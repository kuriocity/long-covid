# 05 — Procedures: PRP, LLLT, and Hair Transplant

Drugs have a ceiling. Once you hit it (12–18 months into a consistent stack), further gain requires a procedure. This is where the money is, so this is where the marketing is worst.

---

## PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma)

### Evidence

The largest synthesis to date — a systematic review/meta-analysis in *Dermatology and Therapy* (2025), **43 RCTs, 1,877 participants**:

- **Activated PRP was effective** at increasing hair density and minimising recurrence versus placebo
- **Non-activated PRP** was associated with **more frequent adverse effects** and less benefit
- Earlier meta-analyses: significant hair-density increase versus placebo at **3 and 6 months**

### The catch

- **The effect is real, modest, and not durable.** It requires maintenance sessions indefinitely
- **Preparation protocols vary enormously** between clinics — centrifugation speed, platelet concentration, activation method, injection depth, session count. This is the main reason results are inconsistent, and you generally cannot verify what your clinic is doing
- Trials used anywhere from 3 sessions at 4-week intervals to 8+ sessions. There is no consensus protocol

### If you do it

- Ask specifically whether the PRP is **activated** (calcium chloride or similar) — the 2025 meta-analysis separates on exactly this
- Standard induction: **3–4 sessions, 4 weeks apart**, then maintenance every **4–6 months**
- India cost: roughly **₹3,000–8,000 per session** depending on city and clinic tier; a first-year course of 4 induction + 2 maintenance sessions lands around **₹18,000–48,000**
- **Position it correctly in the sequence:** PRP is an adjunct for a scalp that still has follicles. It does not restore bald scalp, and it is not a substitute for a 5-ARI. Doing PRP instead of medical therapy is spending more money for less result.

## LLLT (Low-Level Laser Therapy) — caps, combs, helmets

- Several small RCTs are positive; multiple FDA-cleared devices exist (650–680 nm)
- Effect sizes are small, and the trial literature is dominated by device-manufacturer funding
- Device cost: **₹15,000–80,000+** one-time

**Verdict:** defensible as a low-risk add-on if you already own one or the cost is immaterial to you. Poor value as a primary purchase relative to spending the same money on a year of drugs plus a derma pen plus PRP. Not a substitute for anything.

## Hair Transplant (FUE / DHI)

The only intervention that puts hair where there is none. Also the only one that is irreversible.

### The three rules that determine outcome

1. **Never transplant onto an unstabilised scalp.** If native hair is still miniaturising, you transplant 2,500 grafts into the crown and watch the surrounding native hair vanish over the next three years — leaving an island of transplanted hair in a bald field. **Be on a stable 5-ARI + minoxidil regimen for at least 12 months first.** Progression must be halted *before* surgery, not after.

2. **The donor area is a finite, non-renewable budget.** The occipital "safe zone" holds a fixed number of grafts (typically ~5,000–7,000 lifetime for a good donor). Every graft spent is spent forever. This is why surgeons resist transplanting the crown in younger patients — the crown is a **circular, expanding** area that can consume the entire donor supply, and it's the area most likely to keep growing. A hairline is a line; a crown is a hole that gets bigger.

3. **Density expectations must be calibrated.** Native scalp is ~80–100 follicular units/cm². A transplant typically achieves ~35–50 FU/cm² in one pass. That reads as "much better," not "as before." Two passes are often needed for a crown, and that doubles the donor spend.

### Crown-specific considerations

The crown/vertex is the hardest area to transplant well:

- The **whorl pattern** requires precise angle and direction control — a badly angled crown looks obviously surgical
- It expands radially with age, so the surgeon must plan for a **future** larger bald area, not today's
- It's the area that benefits **most** from medical therapy — which is the argument for maximising the drug response for 12–18 months and re-measuring before committing grafts. A crown that fills in 40% on drugs may not need surgery at all

**Standard advice for a moderate crown at a young-to-middle age: medical therapy first, hard, for 12–18 months. Then decide with photos, not with anxiety.**

### FUE vs DHI

- **FUE** — extract follicular units, create recipient channels, place grafts. The workhorse
- **DHI** — implanter-pen variant of FUE; extraction and implantation in one motion, no pre-made channels. Marketed as superior; evidence of a meaningful outcome difference is weak. Typically priced higher
- **The surgeon matters far more than the acronym.** Graft survival is the outcome variable, and it depends on extraction skill, time out of body, handling, and hydration — not the brand name of the technique

### India cost (2026 surveyed ranges)

| Item | Range |
|---|---|
| Per graft, typical FUE | **₹25–60** |
| Per graft, board-certified dermatologist / established surgeon | **₹50–70** |
| Per graft, unregulated / high-volume "budget" clinics | **₹20–40** |
| 2,000 grafts, mid-tier clinic | **₹55,000–80,000** |
| 2,000 grafts, premium clinic | **₹1,00,000–1,40,000** |
| 2,500 grafts (typical crown case) | **₹62,500–1,50,000** |
| Larger sessions / extensive grafting | **₹1,50,000–4,00,000** |

⚠️ **Graft survival is reported around 90%+ at board-certified practices versus roughly 50% at unregulated facilities.** At 50% survival, the cheap clinic is not cheap — you paid half price for half the hair, permanently spent twice the donor grafts to get it, and cannot undo it. **The per-graft price is the least important number in this table.**

Note: these ranges are compiled largely from clinic-published pricing pages, which skew promotional. Treat them as an order of magnitude, not a quote.

### Vetting a surgeon — the actual checklist

- Is the **surgeon** (not a technician) doing the extraction and placement? In many high-volume clinics, technicians do most of the work. Ask directly, and ask who specifically will hold the punch
- **ISHRS membership** or equivalent credentialing
- Ask for **12-month post-op photos of crown cases**, not hairline cases, and not 2-week post-op "density" photos
- Ask for their **graft survival** figure and how they measure it
- Ask what their **long-term plan** for your donor area is, given a crown that will keep expanding. A surgeon who won't discuss the 20-year plan is selling one operation
- **Walk away from anyone who says you don't need finasteride/dutasteride afterward.** It's the clearest single marker of a clinic optimising for booking rather than outcome

## Sequencing summary

```
Month 0     Stabilise: 5-ARI + minoxidil + ketoconazole + microneedling. Baseline photos.
Month 6     First honest read. Adjust the Regrow leg (topical→oral, or add tretinoin).
Month 12    Second read. Progression should be halted; density gain banked.
Month 12-18 Crown still cosmetically insufficient? → PRP course (4 sessions) and re-read at 18 months.
Month 18+   Still insufficient, and scalp demonstrably stable for 12+ months?
            → Transplant consultation. Bring your photo series. Budget donor grafts for a
              crown that will keep expanding. Continue medical therapy permanently, before
              and after surgery.
```

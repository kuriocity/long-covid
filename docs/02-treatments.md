# Long COVID Treatments — Full Evidence Tiers

Compiled from 2025-2026 clinical trials, meta-analyses, and LC specialist protocols.

---

## Tier 1 — Strongest recent RCT evidence

### 1. Fluvoxamine (SSRI) ⭐ newest strong data
- **Trial:** REVIVE (Annals Int Med, March 2026), 399 adults, 60 days, 100 mg BID
- **Result:** Significant fatigue reduction + QoL improvement vs. placebo. Sustained at day 90. FEWER adverse events than placebo.
- **Mechanism:** Sigma-1 receptor agonism (anti-inflammatory) + serotonin restoration
- **Concerns:** CYP1A2/2C19 strong inhibitor — check interactions

### 2. Low-Dose Naltrexone (LDN) ⭐ standard LC clinic first-line
- **Dose:** 1.5–4.5 mg at night (titrate up slowly)
- **Evidence:** ~~2025 meta-analysis (MDPI): 52% respond after 12 weeks~~ **(corrected 2026-09-08 — the 52% comes from Isman 2024, an open-label pilot of LDN + NAD+ iontophoresis, n=36).** Actual state: **no RCT has reported.** 2026 *BMJ Open* meta-analysis pools 4 uncontrolled pre-post studies (n=155): fatigue g −0.74, brain fog g −0.53, sleep −0.60, low certainty. Stanford retrospective (n=59): 54% improved, 32% worsened, **no dose–response**. First placebo-controlled RCT (Vancouver, NCT05430152, n=160) completed Feb 2026 — unpublished.
- **Mechanism:** Reduces microglial activation, immunomodulatory, safe long-term
- **Access:** Requires compounding pharmacy prescription. **India (2026-09-08):** naltrexone 50 mg is sold (Nalcovig ₹78, Addline ₹80, Nodict ₹117) but **no licensed LDN compounding pharmacy was verified**; home dilution (50 mg in 50 mL water = 1 mg/mL) is discouraged by the LDN Research Trust (stability ~3 days refrigerated, uneven dosing). Prescriber decision
- **When to consider:** Good add-on if bupropion alone insufficient

### 3. Guanfacine + N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) ⭐ most brain-fog-specific
- **Yale protocol (Arnsten et al.):** Guanfacine 1 mg at bedtime → titrate to 2 mg after 1 month + NAC 600 mg BID
- **Result:** 8 of 12 patients had cognitive improvement (working memory, concentration, executive function)
- **Mechanism:** Guanfacine = α2A-adrenoceptor agonist restores PFC signaling; NAC blocks KYNA production
- **Watch for:** Hypotension, dizziness from guanfacine (start low). 2/12 in the Yale series stopped for hypotension
- **India (2026-09-08):** guanfacine is **not marketed domestically** (named-patient import only). Clonidine (Arkamin ₹3/tab) is the available α2 agonist but is more sedating and hypotensive with weaker post-synaptic α2A selectivity (Arnsten) — a poor substitute. NAC alone is cheap (Mucinac 600, ₹33)
- **When to consider:** Reserve for if cognitive symptoms dominate later

### 4. Antihistamine combo (H1 + H2)
- **Combo:** Famotidine 20-40 mg BID + fexofenadine 180 mg OR loratadine 10 mg daily
- **Evidence:** ~~STIMULATE-ICP RCT (2026 Lancet Infect Dis) — fatigue improvements~~ **(precised 2026-09-08):** STIMULATE-ICP (Wall 2026, *Lancet Infect Dis*, n=778, **open-label**; famotidine 40 + loratadine 10 daily × 12 wk): Fatigue Assessment Scale **−1.48 vs no-drug (95% CI −2.88 to −0.08; p=0.038)** — statistically significant, **below the ~4-point clinically meaningful change, and no longer different at 24 weeks** after stopping. Case series: Salvucci 2023 (fexofenadine 180 + famotidine 40 × 20 d, n=14 vs 13): brain fog improved 57% (complete 43%), fatigue 79%, complete resolution of all symptoms 29%; Glynne 2022 (n=49): 72% improved. **The food-reactive subgroup has never been trialled.**
- **When to try:** Highest yield if any MCAS features (flushing, food sensitivities, hives, worse-after-eating)
- **When to consider:** Skip if no MCAS features present
- **Full protocol — mechanism, stabilisers (ketotifen), gut repair, India brands/prices, tests, and the blocker challenge-test design:** [05-mast-cell-gut-protocol.md](05-mast-cell-gut-protocol.md)

## Tier 2 — Promising, earlier evidence

### ⭐ Gut synbiotic, Bifidobacterium-based (SIM01) — added 2026-09-08
- **Trial:** Lau 2024, *Lancet Infect Dis* (Hong Kong RECOVERY): double-blind RCT, **n=463**, SIM01 (Bifidobacteria + GOS/XOS/resistant dextrin, 10 bn CFU BID) × 6 months
- **Result:** odds of improvement — **fatigue OR 2.27, memory 1.97, concentration 2.64, GI upset 2.00, general unwellness 2.36**; adverse events = placebo. **The only adequately powered positive gut-directed RCT in LC.** Single centre, sponsor-linked product, unreplicated
- **Also:** VSL#3 450 bn/day × 4 wk (Amoroso 2026, n=48 RCT): Chalder fatigue −24% vs −6%, responders 68% vs 36%. L-glutamine 5 g TID × 8 wk in post-infectious IBS-D with proven hyperpermeability (Zhou 2019 *Gut*, n=106): responders 79.6% vs 5.8% — not LC, but the closest barrier-repair RCT
- **Access:** SIM01 does not ship to India and retail sachets carry 10–30% of the trial dose. Closest clean Indian approximation: Darolac (B. longum 2.5 bn/cap, no L. casei/bulgaricus) 2 caps/day; VSL#3 is sold in India (₹38/cap) but contains L. bulgaricus/paracasei. Full menu in [05-mast-cell-gut-protocol.md](05-mast-cell-gut-protocol.md) §4
- ⚠️ **Caveat on the serotonin rationale:** the human plasma-serotonin depletion finding (Wong 2023, *Cell*) has been challenged on assay grounds (Anderson 2024) and failed replication in two cohorts. The mouse mechanism stands; the human biomarker does not. Do not justify gut repair by "restoring serotonin"


### 5. Nicotinamide Riboside (NR) / NAD+ precursors
- **Trial:** 2025 Lancet eClinicalMedicine RCT, 2000 mg/day, 24 weeks
- **Result:** Within-group improvements in fatigue, mood, sleep at 10 weeks (between-group NS)
- **Access:** OTC (Niagen, Tru Niagen brands)
- **When to consider:** Good — targets mitochondrial recovery

### 6. Mitochondrial supplement stack
- **CoQ10 (ubiquinol form):** 200–400 mg
- **D-ribose:** 5g x 3/day (ME/CFS evidence)
- **L-carnitine:** helps with serotonin restoration too
- **Magnesium:** 300-400 mg glycinate
- **B-complex (methylated):** for methylation
- **Creatine monohydrate:** 5g/day (muscle + brain ATP + cognition)

### 7. IVIG (Intravenous Immunoglobulin)
- **Evidence:** 2026 Lancet Infect Dis case: dramatic cognitive/autonomic improvement, 400-500 mg/kg cycles
- **Best if:** GPCR autoantibody positive (Cell Trend Germany lab)
- **Access:** Expensive, insurance-dependent, being tested in RECOVER-AUTONOMIC
- **When to consider:** Only when labs support autoantibody-driven mechanism

### 8. Nicotine patch (7 mg, transdermal, short course)
- **No RCTs yet.** Case series only. ~20% report ≥50% improvement, 30-40% milder.
- **Protocol:** 6-10 days on, then stop (avoids dependence)
- **Risks:** Anxiety, tachycardia, autonomic overstim — bad if POTS
- **When to consider:** Skip if POTS or autonomic overactivation symptoms present

## Tier 3 — Symptom-specific adjuncts

### For fatigue/wakefulness (only if PEM absent — stimulants can worsen PEM crashes)
- **Modafinil** 100–200 mg AM — great add-on to bupropion
- **Armodafinil** — longer acting
- **Methylphenidate** — if bupropion + modafinil insufficient

### For sleep quality
- **Glycine 3g** before bed (RCT evidence)
- **Magnesium glycinate 300-400 mg** at night
- **L-theanine 200 mg** — calm without sedation
- **Low-dose trazodone** or **mirtazapine** if severe insomnia (Rx)
- **Melatonin 0.3-1 mg** (not the high doses, low dose better)

### For autonomic (POTS/dysautonomia)
- **Ivabradine** — heart rate control
- **Low-dose beta-blockers** (propranolol)
- **Salt + electrolytes** — 2-3g sodium, 3-4g potassium daily
- **Compression garments** (waist-high, 20-30 mmHg)
- **Midodrine** — vasoconstriction if severe orthostatic

## Tier 4 — Experimental / "curative" candidates

### Stellate Ganglion Block (SGB) ⭐ closest thing to a reset button
- **Procedure:** Local anesthetic injection into sympathetic nerve bundle in neck (ultrasound-guided)
- **Effect:** "Reboots" dysregulated autonomic nervous system
- **Evidence (updated 2026-09-08):** largest series — Chiang 2025 (*Cureus*), 102 treated: **55.8% improved**; brain fog 66%, fatigue 58%, headache 81%, PEM 44%; durability of benefit: hours 6%, days 10%, weeks 4%, **months 23%, persistent 13.5%**; **adverse events 25%** (transient headache, worse fatigue, site pain); mean 3 blocks (contralateral at 1–2 wk, repeat at 2–3 months). 2026 systematic review of 7 uncontrolled studies: 56–100% response. Duricka 2025 prospective pilot (n=10, 6 blocks over 3 wk, authors own the clinic). **All uncontrolled.** Two sham-controlled RCTs started 2026: Toronto NCT07468604 (n=78) and Québec NCT07374562 (n=40) — results 2027; RECOVER-TLC Block-LC in development.
- **Access:** Available NOW via interventional pain physicians / anesthesiologists (originally used for PTSD)
- **Cost:** $500-$2000/injection. **India (2026-09-08):** ultrasound-guided SGB is performed at Jaslok Hospital Mumbai (CRPS, PTSD), AIIMS New Delhi pain clinic, Shivam Hospital Ahmedabad and a Karnataka centre — **none advertises it for long COVID and no INR procedure price could be verified** (only ₹600–1,000 consultation fees)
- **When to consider:** ⭐⭐⭐ Highest priority experimental option for the vagal/autonomic-dominant residual phenotype (anhedonia, flat affect, hypersomnia, low HRV, POTS)
- **How to find:** Search "stellate ganglion block long COVID [your city]" or "SGB PTSD [your city]" (originally developed for PTSD)
- **Key:** Must be ultrasound-guided, not blind

### BC-007 (Rovunaptabin) — ~~Phase 2 ongoing~~ ❌ **FAILED** (corrected 2026-09-07)
- **Mechanism:** Aptamer that neutralizes GPCR autoantibodies
- ~~**Trial:** Berlin Cures, Phase 2 ongoing (reCOVer trial completed 2024)~~ **(superseded)**
- **Actual result:** Berlin Cures announced **no superiority over placebo** (Nov 2024). Published as the reCOVer Phase IIa RCT in *eClinicalMedicine* 2025. n=114 across 14 centres in Finland, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Spain
- **Nuance:** a *secondary* endpoint showed significant improvement in self-reported fatigue severity and quality of life. Commentators argue the trial cast too wide a net and wasn't designed to capture responders. Possible, but the primary endpoint was negative — treat as a failed trial, not a pending one
- **Relevance:** downgraded. Not an option

### HELP Apheresis + triple anticoagulation
- **Procedure:** Blood filtration removes fibrinaloid microclots + anticoag (clopidogrel + apixaban + aspirin)
- **Access:** Specialized German clinics
- **Cost:** ~$10,000 USD per course (3-6 sessions typical)
- **Best fit:** "Microclots dominant" phenotype (breathlessness, muscle pain, cognitive fatigue)
- **When to consider:** For microclot-dominant phenotypes (breathlessness, exercise-triggered muscle pain, cognitive fatigue with clot-consistent labs)

### Immunoadsorption
- Similar to apheresis, removes antibodies specifically
- Available Germany, ~$10K/course
- For autoantibody-mediated cases only

### Anti-SARS-CoV-2 Monoclonal Antibodies
- **AER002** trial (UCSF, ends July 2026)
- Directly targets persistent virus
- **Ensitrelvir** (antiviral) — trial ends Dec 2026
- Only relevant if viral persistence confirmed

### Fecal Microbiota Transplant (FMT)
- Targets gut dysbiosis + gut viral reservoir
- Small studies show benefit
- Experimental in most countries

## Tier 5 — 2026 literature sweep (added 2026-09-07)

**Framing first.** A 2026 systematic review concludes that **no pharmacological therapy has demonstrated consistent efficacy in adequately powered RCTs for long COVID.** Nothing below is a cure or a holistic fix. This is consistent with what [04-unified-model.md](04-unified-model.md) predicts for a self-sustaining loop: single agents underperform.

### ⭐ Dopamine-targeted therapy (rasagiline + tyramine-based precursor) — MOST PHENOTYPE-RELEVANT
- **Basis:** the same PET imaging work cited in [01-triad-model.md](01-triad-model.md) — reduced dopamine nerve terminal density correlating with fatigue, motor slowing and memory problems
- **Status:** **patent application filed January 2026**; trial planned, not yet run. ⚠️ **No efficacy data in LC whatsoever**
- **Why it matters here:** rasagiline is an approved generic MAO-B inhibitor (Parkinson's) that preserves dopamine by blocking its breakdown. Available in India. Of everything in the 2026 sweep, this is the only candidate aimed directly at the **dopaminergic deficit phenotype** rather than at inflammation or immunity generally
- ⚠️ **Interaction:** MAO-B inhibitors + SSRIs (fluvoxamine) or bupropion carry serotonin-syndrome caution. Choosing this axis would foreclose the fluvoxamine route
- **Action:** watch only. Not a treatment to request

### 🗓️ Bezisterim (ADDRESS-LC) — near-term catalyst
- **Mechanism:** oral, crosses blood–brain barrier; modulates ERK / NFκB / TNF-α neuroinflammatory pathways
- **Trial:** BioVie Phase 2, ~200 participants, targeting **brain fog and fatigue** specifically
- **Topline results expected September 2026** — set a calendar check

### RECOVER-TLC actives (results expected end-2026)
| Drug | Trial | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Baricitinib** | REVERSE-LC, 17 US sites | JAK inhibitor. The properly-conducted version of the "should I take an immunosuppressant" question — trial-only, not off-label |
| **Semaglutide** | Tame-LC | GLP-1 RA; neuroinflammation hypothesis |
| **LDN** | RECOVER-TLC | Already Tier 1 here |
| **SGB** | RECOVER-TLC | Already Tier 4 here |

### Oxaloacetate (anhydrous enol-oxaloacetate)
- **Evidence:** 4 peer-reviewed publications, ~270 participants, incl. 2 randomized double-blind controlled trials, in ME/CFS and long COVID
- **Access:** OTC supplement (Terra Biological "Oxaloacetate CFS", benaGene)
- ⚠️ **Manufacturer-promoted evidence base — discount accordingly.** Cheapest new item in this sweep

### AXA1125 (endogenous metabolic modulator)
- Phase 2a, n=41, fatigue-predominant LC: **significant reduction in Chalder Fatigue score at day 28** vs placebo. Phosphocreatine recovery and 6MWT did not differ
- **Developer wound down — likely unobtainable.** Listed for completeness

### HBOT (hyperbaric oxygen) — mixed, do not prioritise
- **Positive:** RCT with **40 daily sessions** showed cognitive/fatigue/sleep/pain improvement, sustained at >1-year follow-up
- **Negative:** **HOT-LoCO (2025), n=80, placebo-controlled, double-blind, 10 sessions — no significant difference vs placebo** at 13 weeks
- **Read:** possibly a dose–response effect (40 vs 10 sessions), possibly placebo. ~10 RCTs exist with inconsistent results. **Expensive; low priority**

### taVNS — COVIVA trial (2026)
- Randomized, placebo-controlled pilot, n=45, three arms (sham / sub-threshold / above-threshold), 4 weeks. *Neurology and Therapy*, 2026
- ~~⚠️ Outcome not retrieved in this sweep~~ **Result (read 2026-09-08): NULL.** All three arms improved over 4 weeks on MFI-20 with **no significant difference between sham and either active arm** (25 Hz / 250 µs, ~4 h/day, >80% adherence; AEs: skin irritation 7%, vertigo 7%). Consistent with Percin 2025 (n=42, sham beat active), the Vienna 3-arm trial (n=36, all arms equal) and the Mayo cervical nVNS trial (null). 2026 SR: very-low certainty. **taVNS is downgraded — see Non-drug modalities below**
- Relevant because taVNS is already listed under non-drug modalities below

### Sipavibart (AZD3152)
- Long-acting anti-spike monoclonal targeting **viral persistence**. Klimas group (Nova Southeastern), n=100, multi-year, ongoing
- ⚠️ **Timing caveat:** antivirals and mAbs work best given early. Most viral-persistence evidence concerns the first year; trial participants (and this case, 5 years post-onset) are well beyond that window. Early Paxlovid and mAb trials in LC have already failed

## Dopamine axis after bupropion palpitations — ranked (added 2026-09-08)

For the dopamine-deficit / hypersomnia phenotype when bupropion SR 150 BID produced palpitations (resolved on stopping; modafinil + heavy caffeine were stacked the same week). Prerequisite for anything sympathomimetic: **normal 12-lead ECG + 24 h Holter.** One agent at a time, outside the 5-ARI window.

| Rank | Agent | Evidence | Cardiac / phenotype notes | India |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LDN 1.5 → 4.5 mg** at night | No RCT; 4 uncontrolled studies pooled g −0.74 fatigue, −0.53 brain fog; Vancouver RCT unpublished | No cardiac signal; vivid dreams/insomnia ~10%; broadest mechanism (microglia, immune) | Access is the problem — see Tier 1 #2 |
| 2 | **Bupropion XL 150 once daily** (rechallenge) | Label: palpitations **dose-related** (2% at 300 mg/d, 6% at 400 mg/d, 2% placebo); XL vs SR bioequivalent at equal daily dose, Tmax 5 h vs 3 h. 150 XL = **half** the previous 300 mg/day | **No published rechallenge guidance** — a cardiologist/psychiatrist decision, never with caffeine loading or modafinil | **Bupron XL 150 (Sun) ₹22; Buropin XL ₹14** — a true once-daily XL exists |
| 3 | Amantadine 100 mg BID | Harandi 2024 open-label RCT (n=62, 2 wk): fatigue severity 53→28 vs 50→43; MS-fatigue RCT null; Ohio State placebo RCT completed Dec 2025, unreported | Livedo reticularis ~8%, oedema; QT caution → ECG first | Amantrel ₹18.5; Parkitidin ₹18 |
| 4 | Methylphenidate 10 mg BID | CFS RCT (Blockmans 2006, n=60): responders 17% fatigue / 22% concentration; post-COVID central hypersomnia cases 3/3 responded | Sympathomimetic — same caution as bupropion | Schedule X (Addwize ₹16); in-person only |
| 5 | Modafinil 100–200 mg | **No LC efficacy data**; RECOVER-SLEEP (n=361) completed Mar 2026, unreported; MS-fatigue RCT null | Palpitations 2% vs 1%, tachycardia 2% vs 1%; was on board during the palpitation episode | Modalert 100 ₹25 |

## What DOESN'T work (based on recent evidence)

| Failed intervention | Why |
|---|---|
| **Metformin for existing LC** | REVIVE trial (2026): no benefit (only helps prevent LC if given during acute infection) |
| **Extended Paxlovid (15-25 days)** | RECOVER-VITAL: no significant improvement in PEM, cognition, or orthostatic intolerance |
| **Standard SSRIs (sertraline, escitalopram)** | Symptom relief without root cause change; emotional blunting worsens anhedonia |
| **Steroids alone** | Mask inflammation, don't fix structural damage |
| **Graded exercise therapy (GET) for PEM patients** | Can trigger crashes; only safe for non-PEM phenotype |

## Non-drug modalities (high yield, low risk)

### Vagus nerve stimulation — ⚠️ downgraded 2026-09-08
- ~~**Protocol:** Twice daily 30-min sessions (Italian 2025 study protocol)~~ ~~**Effect:** Autonomic improvement, fatigue reduction, cognitive benefit~~ **(superseded — four sham/active-controlled trials are null: COVIVA 2026 n=45, Percin 2025 n=42, Vienna 2025 n=36, Mayo nVNS n=18. Uncontrolled single-arm studies report within-group gains only.)**
- **Devices:** Nurosym and Pulsetto **do not ship to India**. Biothm VNTune EZ ear clip (Amazon.in) has a 25 Hz / 200 µs preset; generic TENS ear-clip electrodes exist. Research parameters: left cymba conchae, 25 Hz, 250 µs, just below discomfort, 30–60 min 1–2×/day
- **Position:** cheap and safe; expect little. Not a reason to delay anything else

### Cold exposure
- Cold shower 60-180 sec (start with 30 sec, build up)
- Ice bath / cold plunge 2-3 min, 3-4x/week
- Face-only cold immersion daily (bowl of ice water, 15-30 sec)
- **Effect:** 250% dopamine surge, norepinephrine, vagal tone, anti-inflammatory

### Heat exposure (sauna)
- 20 min at 80-90°C, 3-4x/week
- **Effect:** Heat shock proteins, cardiovascular reconditioning, antidepressant (comparable to SSRIs in some studies), reduced inflammation
- Start slow: 10 min first sessions

### Breathwork protocols
- **Resonant breathing:** 5 sec in, 5 sec out (5.5 breaths/min) x 10 min — vagal
- **Extended exhale:** 4 in, 8 out x 5 min — parasympathetic activation
- **Physiological sigh:** 2 quick nose inhales, long mouth exhale — acute stress relief
- **Wim Hof:** 30 fast breaths, hold, 15 sec inhale hold, x3 rounds — morning energizing

### Other vagal exercises
- **Humming/chanting:** 5 min daily
- **Gargling water** vigorously 2x/day, 30 sec
- **Cold face immersion** (mammalian dive reflex)

## Example decision tree — dopamine-deficit / vagal-autonomic residual phenotype

For patients with partial recovery who plateau with anhedonia, hypersomnia, flat affect, and no PEM crashes:

```
Start:
├── Get lab panel first (thyroid, ferritin, testosterone, D, cortisol, EBV)
├── Establish lifestyle foundation (sunlight, sleep, exercise, protein)
├── Add supplement stack (L-tyrosine, NAC, D3, omega-3, glycine, etc.)
│
├── After 4 weeks lifestyle only:
│   └── If still stuck → Discuss bupropion 150 XL with physician
│
├── After 8 weeks bupropion at 300 XL:
│   ├── If working → Continue + optimize
│   └── If plateaued:
│       ├── Add modafinil for hypersomnia component
│       ├── Add LDN for residual neuroinflammation
│       └── Consider SGB (stellate ganglion block)
│
└── After 6 months full stack still plateaued:
    ├── Get GPCR autoantibody panel (Cell Trend, Germany)
    ├── If positive → IVIG or await BC-007 availability
    └── If negative → Continue optimized management + await new trial data
```

Different phenotypes (PEM-dominant, MCAS-dominant, POTS-dominant, cognitive-dominant) call for different starting protocols. This tree is one example, not a universal recommendation.

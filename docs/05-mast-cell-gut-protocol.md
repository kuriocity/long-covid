# 05 — Mast-Cell / Gut Reactivity Protocol (histamine-reactive long COVID)

**Added 2026-09-08.** Companion to [01-triad-model.md](01-triad-model.md) (which tissues) and [04-unified-model.md](04-unified-model.md) (why it stays on). Scope: the long COVID phenotype in which symptoms — here, brain fog — flare **within minutes of eating specific foods**, with no hives or flushing. Everything below was verified against primary sources on 2026-09-08; anything that could not be verified is labelled. India availability and prices are included because every option here had to be buyable.

> Not medical advice. This is a working synthesis to take to a physician. Evidence tiers are stated for every claim.

---

## 0. Summary

- **Reframe.** The trigger list (fermented milk drink, black tea, pineapple, hot chocolate, *expired* fish oil) is mostly **histamine liberators and irritants, not histamine-rich foods**. Refined fish oil contains no histidine, so histamine cannot form in it as it ages; what forms is lipid peroxides and aldehydes. Pineapple, cocoa and tea contain little histamine. Only the fermented drink is a real exogenous-histamine source. So the working diagnosis is **hyper-reactive gut mucosal mast cells signalling through sensitised vagal afferents**, of which "histamine intolerance / DAO deficiency" is at most one sub-mechanism. This matters: DAO enzyme supplements are irrelevant, receptor blockade alone may be partial, and **mast-cell stabilisation plus barrier repair plus trigger removal** is the lever set.
- **Anatomy explains the minutes.** In human gut mucosa 47–78% of mast cells sit apposed to nerve fibres; vagal afferents carry H1 and 5-HT3 receptors and fire within ~1 minute of a luminal trigger; the vagus is the route by which sickness signals reach the brainstem. Histamine itself does not cross the blood–brain barrier. The fog is a **neural reflex** (plus possibly a blood-pressure dip), not "histamine in the brain."
- **Evidence honesty.** The only RCT of H1+H2 blockers in long COVID (STIMULATE-ICP, *Lancet Infect Dis* July 2026, n=778) found a **small, statistically significant, non-sustained** fatigue benefit in *unselected* patients. No trial has tested a food-reactive subgroup. Case series report 57–79% improvement in brain fog and fatigue but are tiny and retrospective. Ketotifen and oral cromolyn have **no** long COVID data at all. This protocol is therefore an **n-of-1 experiment with a fast, specific readout**, not an evidence-based treatment.
- **Gut repair has one real RCT.** The SIM01 Bifidobacterium synbiotic (n=463, 6 months) roughly doubled the odds of improvement in fatigue, memory, concentration and gut symptoms. It does not ship to India; the closest clean domestic option is approximated below.
- **What the fast readout buys.** A deliberate trigger challenge with and without blockers gives a same-day, placebo-resistant answer. Run it first.

## 1. Anatomy and mechanism

### 1.1 The gut mast cell–vagus synapse

| Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 47.1–77.7% of mucosal mast cells in human GI biopsies are directly apposed to nerves | Stead 1989, *Gastroenterology* | Human histology |
| In IBS, mast cells within 5 µm of nerves are ~3× more numerous and correlate with symptom severity | Barbara 2004, *Gastroenterology* | Human biopsies |
| Mesenteric afferent discharge rises **~1 minute** after a luminal trigger; blocked by an H1 antagonist (pyrilamine) and a 5-HT3 antagonist | Jiang 2000, *Gastroenterology* (rat) | Animal electrophysiology |
| Vagal mucosal afferents are co-sensitive to histamine (H1) and serotonin (5-HT3) | Kreis 2002, *Am J Physiol GI* (rat) | Animal |
| Gastric mast cells adjacent to nerve terminals → vagal hypersensitivity → amygdala CRH↑/BDNF↓ → anxiety/depression-like behaviour; **vagotomy normalised behaviour; ketotifen partially reversed it** | Cordner 2021, *JCI Insight* (rat) | Animal |
| Sub-diaphragmatic vagotomy blocks sickness behaviour from a gut immune stimulus | Bluthé 1994; Dantzer 2008 review | Animal, landmark |
| Oral histamine 75 mg produced symptoms in 5/10 volunteers with **no rise in plasma histamine** | Wöhrl 2004, *Allergy Asthma Proc* | Human provocation |
| Circulating histamine does not cross an intact blood–brain barrier | Alstadhaug 2014; Haas 2008 | Reviews |

**Reading:** a food that degranulates mucosal mast cells (or directly irritates afferents) produces a brainstem-relayed "sickness" signal within a minute or two, without histamine reaching the brain. That is exactly the observed time course. A second, additive route is systemic: H1/H2-mediated vasodilation → a transient blood-pressure dip → cerebral hypoperfusion. A home BP cuff at 0/15/30 min separates the two.

### 1.2 Why "histamine intolerance" is too narrow

- **DAO (diamine oxidase)** is made by mature enterocytes at the villus tips and acts **in the lumen** on *ingested* histamine. It is acid-labile as a supplement and not systemically active. It cannot address liberators, irritants, non-histamine mediators (PAF, PGD2, tryptase, leukotrienes) or neural signalling (Jackson 2025 review; Reese 2021 guideline: DAO supplementation "not recommended").
- Of ten published low-histamine food lists, only **32%** of excluded foods are justified by measured histamine content; the liberator mechanism itself is "not yet elucidated" (Sánchez-Pérez 2021, *Nutrients*).
- Serum DAO has "no diagnostic value" (Reese 2021); it stayed flat in patients who improved on diet (Wagner 2017; Son 2018).

### 1.3 Brain histamine — why sedating antihistamines are the wrong tool here

Brain histamine neurons live only in the tuberomammillary nucleus and are **wake-promoting**. A brain-penetrant H1 blocker therefore causes sedation — the wrong direction for a hypersomnia phenotype. PET receptor-occupancy data (Yanai/Tashiro group):

| Drug | Brain H1 receptor occupancy | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Fexofenadine 120 mg | **−0.1% (nil)** — Tashiro 2004; Ansotegui 2024 SR: 0% | Non-sedating |
| Bilastine 20 mg | Non-brain-penetrating; no driving impairment at 20–40 mg | Non-sedating |
| Cetirizine 20 mg | **26%** (Tashiro 2004); somnolence risk difference 6.5% in RCTs without run-in (Du 2016) | Less-sedating |
| Levocetirizine | Somnolence RR 1.67 vs placebo (Snidvongs 2017, n=18,014) | Less-sedating |
| Ketotifen | **>50%** (first-generation) | Sedating |

Thresholds: <20% non-sedating, 20–50% less-sedating, ≥50% sedating (Kawauchi & Yanai 2019). **Consequence: daytime H1 blockade = fexofenadine or bilastine only.** Ketotifen, if used, goes at night and is judged partly by whether daytime sleepiness worsens.

### 1.4 Stress, CRF and the vagal brake — where the gut leg meets the vagal leg

| Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Cold-pain stress released tryptase and histamine into the jejunal lumen of healthy volunteers | Santos 1998, *Gastroenterology* | Human in vivo |
| CRH raised permeability of human colonic biopsies ~2×; abolished by a mast-cell stabiliser (lodoxamide); CRH receptors found on lamina-propria mast cells | Wallon 2008, *Gut* | Human tissue |
| Public-speaking stress raised small-bowel permeability ~2× in vivo; **disodium cromoglycate blocked it** | Vanuytsel 2014, *Gut* | Human in vivo |
| Cholinergic (α7-nicotinic) signalling suppresses antigen-induced mast-cell degranulation | Kageyama-Yahara 2008 (mouse) | Animal |
| Vagal signalling can also *drive* mesenteric mast cells via muscarinic receptors | Woestemeier 2026 (mouse) | Animal, conflicting |
| Human evidence that vagal tone or VNS suppresses mast-cell degranulation | — | **NOT FOUND** |

**Reading:** the stress axis (CRF) degranulates gut mast cells in humans; a stabiliser blocks it. This is the anatomical junction of the "gut" and "vagus" legs in [01-triad-model.md](01-triad-model.md) — one circuit, not two. It is also why sleep, stress load and alcohol move the threshold day to day.

### 1.5 What the 2026 tissue data show — and don't

- **Augustin 2026, *Mucosal Immunol*** — terminal-ileum biopsies 15–22 months post-infection: post-COVID-syndrome patients had **higher ileal mast-cell activity, higher serum tryptase (p=0.02), higher zonulin, and more tissue spike/nucleocapsid protein** than recovered controls (n≈20 vs 23). This is the first direct human evidence linking gut mast-cell activation to persistent viral protein in long COVID.
- **Lenning 2024, *Scand J Immunol*** — serum β-tryptase and CPA3 **did not differ** between 24 long COVID and 24 recovered patients. So **a normal baseline serum tryptase does not exclude tissue-level activity.**
- Spike protein (not RBD) drives human mast cells to release IL-1β, chymase and tryptase via TLR4 and ACE2 (Tsilioni & Theoharides 2023, in vitro); spike-RBD triggers rapid degranulation in hACE2 mice, and stabilisers prevented the downstream injury (Wu 2021, 2024). Mechanism is robust; human treatment data are not.
- The counter-view: patients meeting *strict* (ECNM-AIM) MCAS criteria have never been shown to overlap with long COVID (Lee & Picard 2025). The symptom-survey overlap (Weinstock 2021: long COVID symptom scores indistinguishable from MCAS patients) is real but self-selected and non-specific.

**Famotidine tachyphylaxis (verified):** for *gastric acid* endpoints, ~11% efficacy loss by day 3 and ~20% by day 15, then plateau (Clark 2026 scoping review); a drug-free interval preserved the response over 5 months (Nwokolo 1991). **Fermented-food paradox:** high fermented-food intake raises microbiome diversity and lowers inflammatory markers (Wastyk 2021, *Cell*) but is the main dietary histamine source. During the elimination phase, take the probiotic capsule instead of the fermented food; reintroduce ferments last.

**Low-FODMAP overlap:** a 3-week low-FODMAP diet cut urinary histamine **eightfold** in IBS (McIntosh 2017, *Gut*) — fermentable carbohydrate load feeds histamine-producing bacteria. Legumes, onion/garlic, wheat and lactose loads matter as well as the SIGHI list.

## 5. Diet protocol (Reese 2021 guideline + SIGHI)

| Phase | Duration | What |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Elimination** | Guideline: 10–14 days; SIGHI: 2–6 weeks | Fresh-cooked only. No leftovers, canned, fermented, aged, cured, smoked, vinegar, alcohol. Avoid liberators (pineapple, papaya, citrus, strawberry, tomato, spinach, eggplant, chocolate/cocoa, nuts, shellfish). Refrigerate protein foods immediately; freeze leftovers, do not refrigerate them |
| **2. Reintroduction** | Up to 6 weeks | One food at a time, 2 consecutive days, 1–2 day gap; repeat each test at least twice; only test while symptom-free. Fog log 0–10 at 0/15/30/60 min |
| **3. Individualised maintenance** | Ongoing | Eat everything tolerated; re-test moderately tolerated foods every few months |

Rules that matter more than the list: **freshness** (histamine forms in stored protein food), **no alcohol** (liberator + DAO inhibitor), and **not staying in Phase 1** — restriction is diagnostic, not the treatment. SIGHI leaflet: <https://www.histaminintoleranz.ch/downloads/SIGHI-Leaflet_HistamineEliminationDiet.pdf>; SIGHI itself notes that for mast-cell-mediated cases the DAO list alone "is not sufficient."

## 7. The challenge test (n-of-1 design)

The whole hypothesis can be tested in two days, and the result is placebo-resistant because the trigger produces a fast, specific, reproducible symptom.

1. **Day A — unblocked.** Same trigger, same quantity (the fermented milk drink or 100 g pineapple), empty stomach, seated. Rate fog 0–10 and take BP/HR at 0, 15, 30, 60 min. Write it down.
2. **Day B — blocked.** Fexofenadine 180 mg + famotidine 40 mg **one hour before** the identical trigger. Same ratings.
3. **Interpretation.** Fog abolished or clearly blunted on Day B → histamine (H1/H2) is a major mediator. Fog unchanged → the reaction is via non-histamine mediators or direct afferent irritation → move to a stabiliser (ketotifen) and barrier repair; H1/H2 alone will not fix it. Systolic drop ≥20 mmHg on Day A → a postprandial-hypotension component is present.
4. Repeat each arm once if the result is ambiguous. A single dose does not confound any other trial running in the background.

## 9. Sequencing with the 5-ARI and dopaminergic questions

The confound rule ([../mpb/docs/07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md](../mpb/docs/07-5ari-neuro-overlap.md)) is about **changes**, not steady states. A stable background stack does not confound a later single change. So:

1. **Weeks 0–6 — quiet the gut/mast-cell input.** Challenge test in week 1 (same-day answer). Daily stack from week 1, elimination diet weeks 1–4, reintroduction weeks 4–6. Readout: the post-prandial fog log, which is separate from the weekly anhedonia/drive ratings.
2. **Weeks 6–18 — the 5-ARI change, stack held constant.** Weekly mood/drive/libido ratings per doc 07. Antihistamines have at most a ~1.5-point fatigue effect in RCT data and no plausible effect on libido or anhedonia, so a *stable* histamine stack is an acceptable background for reading the 5-ARI signal. Note: standalone topical finasteride 0.25% is **not sold in India** — only 0.1% + minoxidil combinations (Morr-F etc.); the MPB plan needs an India-feasible step-down chosen before week 6.
3. **Week 18+ — one dopaminergic/energy agent at a time**, after a normal Holter. Ranked in [02-treatments.md](02-treatments.md) §"Dopamine axis after bupropion palpitations."

If the user chooses the reverse (5-ARI first), the histamine challenge test can still run in week 1 — a single dose confounds nothing — and the daily stack starts after the 12-week window.

## 10. What "permanent" would look like — and what is unknown

**Plausible exit path (mechanistic, not trial-proven):** remove triggers 2–6 weeks → stabilise mast cells 8–12 weeks → repair the barrier (glutamine 8 weeks, Bifidobacteria, soluble fibre, vitamin D) → lower CRF/stress drive and rebuild vagal tone (sleep, breathing, exercise, alcohol out) → graded reintroduction to re-establish tolerance → **taper the drugs at 3–6 months to test whether reactivity has settled.** DAO-type intolerance secondary to mucosal damage is reversible when the mucosa heals (the celiac analogy: DAO recovers on a gluten-free diet). Primary MCAS is chronic. A mild, fog-only, no-hives presentation sits at the reversible end.

**Unknown:** whether post-viral mast-cell reactivity resolves on this path (no trial); whether gut viral protein persistence (documented to ~22–30 months, nothing at 4–5 years) still drives it at year five; whether the Augustin tissue findings generalise. The honest expectation is *reduced frequency and severity, then a controlled test of drug withdrawal*, not a promise of eating anything again by a date.

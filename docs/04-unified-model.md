# The Stuck Sickness Program — A Unified Root-Cause Model of Long COVID

*A synthesis connecting every major long COVID hypothesis into one mechanistic chain: why the symptoms occur, why treatments work only partially, and what permanent recovery requires.*

---

## The model in one paragraph

Long COVID symptoms are not damage — they are a **program**. Every animal has an evolved, centrally-coordinated "sickness mode" (Dantzer's sickness behavior), triggered by immune signals and run by the hypothalamus/brainstem, that deliberately produces fatigue, cognitive slowing, anhedonia, hypersomnia, and social/emotional withdrawal to force rest and reallocate energy toward fighting infection. It is supposed to switch off when infection clears. **Long COVID is this program failing to switch off** — because SARS-CoV-2 damages both the *inputs* the brain uses to decide (gut, endothelium, and vagal sensors keep sending "still infected" signals) and the *off-switch hardware itself* (vagal cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, HPA axis). The program is bistable — a latch with hysteresis. Every known treatment dims one input or forces one output, which is why each works partially. Permanent recovery = the latch flipping OFF, which requires quieting all inputs, repairing the signal channel, and actively pushing the system past its exit threshold.

## The three layers

```
LAYER 1 — HARDWARE (the lesions)
   Gut epithelium + vascular endothelium + vagus nerve damaged
   (the Damaged Triad — the ACE2-richest tissues; see 01-triad-model.md)
                    │
                    ▼
LAYER 2 — SIGNAL (corrupted telemetry)
   The brain runs the body on interoceptive data carried mostly by
   the vagus nerve (~80% of its fibers are afferent/sensory).
   Post-COVID this channel is corrupted in BOTH directions:
   • FALSE ALARMS: inflamed vagal sensors + gut LPS + microclot
     debris + GPCR autoantibodies keep reporting "infection ongoing"
   • MISSING ALL-CLEAR: gut serotonin depletion weakens the vagal
     afferent signaling that normally supports calm and cognition
   → The brain is flying on corrupted instruments.
                    │
                    ▼
LAYER 3 — STATE (the stuck program)
   Acting correctly on wrong data, the brain keeps the evolved
   sickness/austerity program latched ON:
   energy-budget triage → the characteristic symptom package
```

### The cellular mirror

The same logic runs at cell level. Mitochondria are threat sensors, not just power plants. Under danger they enter the **Cell Danger Response** (Naviaux): deliberately throttling ATP production and shifting to defense signaling (extracellular ATP / purinergic alarm). ME/CFS metabolomics shows a "dauer-like" hypometabolic signature. The **itaconate shunt hypothesis** (Phair/OMF) describes a concrete cell-autonomous latch: innate-immune signaling diverts the TCA cycle → chronically low ATP → sustained stress signaling → sustained immune state. **Fatigue is not broken engines — it is engines commanded into low-power mode.**

## Why exactly these symptoms — energy-budget triage

The brain's core job is predictive energy budgeting (allostasis). When the ledger reads "infection," it cuts the most expensive discretionary line items first — and reinstates them last:

| Symptom | What the brain actually cut | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fatigue | Global metabolic budget throttled | Cheapest defense: make everything *feel* expensive |
| Brain fog | Prefrontal working memory + astrocyte glutamate cycling | The most ATP-hungry cortical operations |
| Mental exhaustion | Sustained-attention networks | Can't sustain high-frequency oscillations on a throttled budget |
| Anhedonia / no drive | Ventral striatum dopamine — the "is effort worth it?" computation | Dopamine encodes predicted value of energy expenditure; austerity mode appraises every effort as not-worth-it. Consistent with 2026 imaging showing reduced striatal dopamine terminal density in LC |
| Lethargy / slowed movement | Dorsal striatum dopamine (vigor) | Movement vigor is a continuous energy spend |
| Hypersomnia | Orexin/arousal suppression; IL-1β and TNF-α are directly somnogenic | Sleep = cheapest state + immune housekeeping |
| Emotional flatness / no excitement | Full-range affect + anticipatory dopamine | Emotions are energy-mobilization programs; anticipation drives investment — both disabled under austerity |

**Key clinical implication:** the symptom cluster arrives as a package and *departs in reverse order of expense*. Basic function returns first; drive, excitement, and joy — the budget's luxury items — return last, only when the brain is confident the surplus is real. Patients who "recovered but feel nothing" are in late-stage austerity, not permanent deficit.

## Why the program stays stuck — five latch mechanisms

Removing the original trigger isn't enough because the ON state self-stabilizes:

1. **Microglial priming / trained immunity** — brain immune cells become epigenetically locked in high-gain mode; minor triggers now produce major inflammatory responses
2. **Sensor corruption** — damaged vagal afferents mis-report danger (a phantom signal — VanElzakker's vagus-infection hypothesis for ME/CFS); the brain never receives clean "all clear" data
3. **Setpoint drift** — sympathetic-dominant autonomic balance and flattened cortisol rhythm become the recalibrated "normal" (LC cohorts show markedly reduced morning cortisol)
4. **Cellular bistability** — CDR / itaconate-shunt loops: low ATP → cell stress → danger signaling → keep ATP low
5. **Disuse atrophy** — after months-to-years of suppression, dopamine terminals and receptors physically downregulate; late-stage residual symptoms reflect atrophy + setpoint drift more than active suppression

This is textbook **hysteresis**: a latch flipped ON at one threshold must be pushed well past a different threshold to flip OFF.

### Suppression vs. atrophy — the two stages

| Stage | Dominant mechanism | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Early LC (active illness) | Active central suppression by inflammation | Anti-inflammatory / input-removal (LDN, fluvoxamine, antivirals, anticoag) |
| Late residual (functional but flat) | Setpoint drift + reward-circuit atrophy | Forcing functions: dopaminergic drugs + exercise + novelty + circadian anchoring |

## Why every treatment works only partially

Each intervention touches a single arrow in the causal chain — none flips the latch alone:

| Treatment | What it touches | Why partial |
|---|---|---|
| Antivirals (Paxlovid, ensitrelvir) | Removes viral input | Other inputs + hysteresis remain |
| Apheresis / anticoagulation | Removes microclot input | Same |
| IVIG / BC-007 | Removes autoantibody input | Same |
| H1+H2 antihistamines | Dims mast-cell input | Same |
| Fluvoxamine | Dims central inflammation (sigma-1) + serotonin | One node of the amplifier |
| LDN | De-primes microglia (TLR4 antagonism) | Slow; doesn't fix inputs or setpoints |
| NAC | Blocks kynurenine neurotoxicity; restores glutathione | Support, not a switch |
| Guanfacine | Patches prefrontal signaling downstream | Output-side fix |
| Bupropion / stimulants | Forces motivation/arousal output against the program | Ceiling = program still suppressing underneath (unless paired with behavioral re-expansion — see below) |
| Stellate ganglion block (SGB) | Interrupts the stuck sympathetic loop — **hits the latch itself** | Explains occasional dramatic single-shot recoveries; can relapse if inputs remain |
| VNS / breathwork / cold exposure | Slowly repairs telemetry channel + off-switch | Cumulative, not acute |
| Exercise / sunlight / circadian anchoring | Recalibrates setpoints; proves surplus to the budget | Slow; requires the program to permit activity first (PEM patients: not yet) |

**This resolves the field's central puzzle:** single-drug RCTs keep failing or showing modest effects, while multi-modal specialist clinics report much better outcomes. You cannot exit a bistable state by dimming one input.

**It also explains the strange recovery stories** — sudden remission after pregnancy, a second infection, a vaccine dose, or prolonged fasting: massive whole-system perturbations that kick the network across the ridge into the healthy attractor. And why ~60% recover with time alone: inputs decay naturally until the system crosses threshold — often experienced as "it just lifted one day," classic all-or-nothing latch behavior.

## The permanent solution — a derived blueprint

A single permanent pill **cannot exist** under this model, because the root cause is a distributed stable *state*, not a single lesion. But permanent **exit** exists, with a defined shape:

**Phase A — Quiet the remaining inputs.**
Comprehensive labs → treat what's found (thyroid, iron, testosterone, vitamin D). Gut restoration. Phenotype-dependent: antivirals for confirmed persistence, anticoagulation for microclot-dominant cases, antibody clearance (IVIG/BC-007) for autoantibody-positive cases.

**Phase B — Repair the telemetry + off-switch.**
Daily vagal rehabilitation (resonant breathing, cold exposure, humming/gargling, transcutaneous VNS). Circadian anchoring (fixed wake time, morning light) to re-steepen the cortisol curve. SGB for plateaued autonomic-dominant cases — the closest available thing to a hard reboot.

**Phase C — Force the state transition.**
For the dopamine-deficit residual phenotype, this is what bupropion actually *is* in the model — not merely symptomatic: artificially raising dopamine raises the brain's estimate of available resources → effort starts feeling worth it → behavior expands → real positive energy data flows back through the (repaired) telemetry → the budget updates → the program releases. **Drug + behavior together** break the anhedonia→inactivity→anhedonia sub-loop. Drug alone just props a gauge.

**Phase D — Consolidate the healthy attractor.**
6–12 months of graded exercise (demonstrates reserve), novelty/social/reward re-engagement (regrows downregulated dopamine circuitry), stable sleep architecture. Then taper the drug — **if the state holds without it, the latch has genuinely flipped.** That is the operational definition of permanent recovery.

### Measurable dashboard for latch position

| Marker | What it reflects | Direction on recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Morning HRV trend | Vagal telemetry integrity | ↑ |
| Resting heart rate | Sympathetic dominance | ↓ |
| AM cortisol / diurnal slope | HPA setpoint | ↑ / steeper |
| hs-CRP, IL-6 | Residual input level | ↓ |
| Weekly anhedonia self-score | Program grip on reward system | ↑ |

When these move together, the system is crossing the ridge.

## Individual susceptibility (why some people, not others)

Latch threshold varies by: baseline vagal tone/HRV, gut microbiome diversity, autoimmune predisposition (HLA type), sex (female immune reactivity), initial viral load and reservoir size, metabolic/mitochondrial reserve, prior infections (EBV serostatus). Day-to-day symptom fluctuation reflects a system near a basin boundary — small perturbations (poor sleep, stress, alcohol, minor infections) temporarily push it deeper.

## Scientific pillars (real bodies of work this synthesis stands on)

- **Sickness behavior** — Dantzer & Kelley: cytokine-induced coordinated behavioral state
- **Inflammation-induced dopamine suppression** — Miller & Raison: IFN-α therapy induces anhedonia via reduced striatal dopamine
- **Vagus nerve infection hypothesis** — VanElzakker (ME/CFS)
- **Cell Danger Response** — Naviaux: mitochondria as danger sensors; hypometabolic survival states
- **Itaconate shunt hypothesis** — Phair / Open Medicine Foundation (ME/CFS bistability)
- **Gut serotonin–vagal axis in LC** — Levy lab (Cell): viral persistence → serotonin depletion → reduced vagal signaling → cognitive symptoms
- **Low cortisol in LC** — Klein & Iwasaki cohorts
- **Fibrinaloid microclots** — Pretorius & Kell
- **Striatal dopamine terminal loss in LC** — 2026 imaging study
- **Neuroinflammation / BBB disruption** — Nature Neuroscience 2024

The *integration* is the hypothesis — coherent and falsifiable, not yet proven doctrine. See [`sources.md`](sources.md) for links.

## Falsifiable predictions

1. HRV trajectory should predict recovery better than any blood marker
2. SGB response rate should be highest in the autonomic/flat-affect residual phenotype
3. Combined input-removal + vagal-repair protocols should outperform either alone in RCTs
4. Recovery events should show state-transition dynamics (rapid, nonlinear) more often than gradual linear improvement in long-duration patients
5. Anhedonia should resolve after functional capacity, not before, in most patients
6. Bupropion + behavioral activation should outperform bupropion alone specifically in late-stage LC anhedonia
